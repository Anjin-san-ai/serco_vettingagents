What the agents actually read

One candidate, every step, and the document in front of it

The process maps show what happens. They don't show the thing the room always asks about: what does the agent receive, and how does it reach a decision? This walks Davinder Kaur down the 1.1–1.8 onboarding spine — seventeen steps, sixteen documents — and for each one shows the evidence, the fields extracted from it, the rules that fire, and the decision that lands.

Two steps deliberately fail. Those are the interesting ones.

Read the provenance labels. The process on this page is sourced — every step, volume and defect rate carries its slide reference, and steps with no stated SLA say so. The documents are generated specimens, watermarked and carrying deliberately invalid identifiers; no real candidate evidence was used or is reproduced here. The confidence scores and rule thresholds are designed, not measured. Wave 0 of the delivery sequence exists precisely to measure per-document-type accuracy against the human outcome, and that measurement has not happened yet. Treat this as a design walkthrough, which is what it is — not as a claim about accuracy.

The gate

What “a human decides” actually commits us to

Two steps in the walkthrough stop and ask a person, and you can answer them. These are the properties that make that a gate rather than a formality — and most of them are already enforced in the simulation engine rather than merely intended.

One way in, for humans and timers alike

Every release of a held case goes through the same single queue, whether a person clicked or an auto-resume timer fired. The engine has one inbound channel and a test asserts that the timer uses the identical path, so there is no privileged back door that skips the gate.

Enforced by sim.hitl in test/run.js

The agent proposes; it never writes

A4 computes the correction and puts it in front of someone. It does not hold a pen. The corrected date of birth reaches SAP because a named person approved it, which is why the audit step on this page is blocked until you answer step 8.

A hold costs something

A held case occupies its agent for far longer than the agent’s own work takes, and the engine models that back-pressure. Human oversight is not free, and a design that pretended otherwise would under-estimate the queue it creates.

No auto-release on the conviction

A timer may reasonably nudge a stalled data correction. It must never release a special-category decision. The conviction hold has no timer, and the routing guard refuses the default queue even when a human is slow to answer.

Named recipients, not shared queues

Both gates address one identified person, with their role stated. The whole point of the confidentiality guard is that “send it to the team” is the wrong answer, so “ask the team” cannot be the right one either.

Non-response escalates, it does not loop

Persistent silence goes to the vetting officer and the line manager rather than sitting in a chase cycle. A gate nobody answers has to fail upwards, or it becomes the three-day delay it was meant to remove.

Decision logs, retention and the guard set are the Assurance & Audit plane’s remit — see the Agent fleet page. Every answer you give on this page writes a log entry showing the guard, the person and the outcome, in that order.

Still open

Three things the sources don't settle

What is “REV”?

It appears exactly once, in the Check & Progress outputs — “References, DBS, REV, Credit Checks etc.” — and nowhere else in either document. We have not guessed at it on this page, and the evidence pack does not include it.

[WS p2]

How is “the role sits within ER” determined?

The confidentiality rule that blocks the conviction routing at step 12 depends on this test, and the sources state the rule without stating how the condition is evaluated. Until it is, that step has to escalate to a person rather than route automatically.

Recorded as a stated risk on process 1.5

No countermeasure has been implemented yet

The improvement record names three root causes for the 23% pushback and reads “Workshop booked to discuss” against all three. The manager declaration and the verification stamp on this page are proposed controls, badged as such wherever they appear.
