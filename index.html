And the Specialist Vetting process map carries a watermark across the whole page: DRAFT — DON'T AUTOMATE.

Automating the current process would mean automating a process that fails 23% of the time at its front door, and industrialising a decision your own SMEs describe as value-adding human judgement. So this solution is sequenced, and every wave has a gate.

The approach

Prevent, then automate, then support the judgement

01 · Prevent

Move the check left

Today quality is checked after submission, by a human, downstream. Four agents move that check into the manager's hands at the moment of upload: a decision tree for the right document set, digital identity verification with an audit stamp, field extraction, and reconciliation against Appian.

Attacks the 14% / 13% / 6% / 3% root causes. [WS p7]

02 · Automate

Straight-through the transactional middle

SAP filing, field keying and ticket routing are high-volume, rule-based and multi-system — exactly what should not consume a specialist's day. Two agents deliver your three existing business cases, and the audit becomes event-driven on contract signature rather than batch-and-queue.

54 + 43 + 27 hrs/month. [WS p11–p13]

03 · Support

Leave the clearance decision with people

Check & Progress adjudication stays human. An agent assembles the evidence pack — references, DBS, credit, 5-year address history, occupational health — and presents a reasoned recommendation and the gaps. A vetting officer decides. No agent ever issues a clearance.

Higher Level Checks, risk acceptance and ER escalation stay human-authority.

Delivery sequence

Six waves, six gates

Nothing proceeds because a slide says it should. Each wave has to pass a measurable gate before the next begins — starting with the measurement Serco says is missing.
